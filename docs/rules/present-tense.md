# Present-tense rules

## `-isc-` verbs

**Code:** `ISC_VERBS`, `withIsc`, `resolveIsc`, `iscAlternatives`
**Where:** `scripts/corrections.ts`

**What it does:** 17 *-ire* verbs that Morph-it gives only without the *-isc-*
infix (*abbruto*, not *abbrutisco*). The rule adds each verb's *-isc-* forms
(generated by `scripts/regular.ts`) at the present indicative and subjunctive
persons that take it, and the imperative *tu*, then chooses them over
Morph-it's wrong plain forms.

**In plain terms:** some Italian *-ire* verbs insert *-isc-* before the ending
in most persons of the present tense (*io fin-isc-o*, not *io fino*). Morph-it
had simply missed this for these 17 verbs.

**Verbs:** abbrutire, aggrinzire, ammollire, asservire, bramire, brunire,
censire, graffire, grugnire, gualcire, incretinire, inferocire, plaudire,
poltrire, rabbonire, rattrappire, tripartire.

**Exception — `aggrinzire`:** Treccani gives both forms as valid ("io
aggrinzisco o aggrinzo"), so the plain form is kept as a `common` alternative
via `ISC_AND_PLAIN`. For the other 16, the plain form is a mistake, not an
alternative — including `rattrappire`, where Treccani's "e rispettivam. io
rattrappo" reads as belonging to the separate verb *rattrappare*, not as a
valid alternative of *rattrappire*.

**Source:** each verb checked individually on Treccani ("io abbrutisco, tu
abbrutisci, ecc." and equivalents); English Wiktionary agreed for all 17.

**A bug found and fixed here:** `resolveIsc` originally had no restriction on
which tense it applied to, so for a verb that was *also* in
`PARTICIPLE_IENTE_VERBS` (`ammollire`), it could accidentally intercept the
present-participle slot too — its isc-agnostic form computation happened to
match Morph-it's existing wrong candidate there, stealing the decision before
the participle rule ever ran. Fixed by restricting `resolveIsc` to
`ind.pres`/`sub.pres`/`impr.pres` only. The same defensive fix was applied to
`resolveStressedI` below, which had the identical structural risk.

## Stressed-*i* `-iare` verbs

**Code:** `STRESSED_I_VERBS`, `withStressedI`, `resolveStressedI`
**Where:** `scripts/corrections.ts`

**What it does:** 14 *-iare* verbs whose *i* is stressed in the present
(*spìo*, not *spio*), so an ending starting with *i* keeps both letters:
*spii*, *spiino*, not *spi*, *spino*. Morph-it gives the forms of an
unstressed-*i* verb (*studi*, *studino*). The rule adds the stressed-*i*
forms (generated by `regularForms(verb, false, true)`) wherever they differ
from the unstressed pattern, and chooses them.

**In plain terms:** most *-iare* verbs drop one of the two *i*'s when an
ending starts with *i* too (*studiare* → *studi*, not *studii*). A handful
don't, because their own stress falls on that *i* rather than the syllable
before it.

**Verbs:** desiare, deviare, espiare, forviare, fuorviare, obliare, piare,
ravviare, razziare, riavviare, sciare, spiare, striare, sviare.

**`sciare` also extends into the future/conditional** (*scierò*, not
*scerò*) — the only verb in this list where the stressed *i* survives beyond
the present tense.

**`piare`'s `noi` form:** kept as *piamo* (matching Wiktionary and the
regular pattern used for the rest of the family); Hoepli's *piiamo* is kept
as a `rare` alternative in the present indicative only.

**Source:** each verb checked — Treccani marks the stress directly for 10
("io devìo", "io strio, tu strii"); Hoepli for `piare` ("pìo, -pìi, piiàmo");
conjugation tables (not dictionaries) for `riavviare`, `sciare` and `sviare`,
cross-checked against each other; English Wiktionary agreed for all 14.
Morph-it's unstressed forms are rejected outright — they aren't valid
alternatives (*devi* is a form of *dovere*, *spino* is the noun "thorn").
